# Create polished presentations

Create a deck that helps its audience understand and remember the intended message. Keep claims faithful to supplied evidence, and make the file easy to present and edit.

## Plan the story first

1. Identify the audience, goal, setting, requested duration or slide count, and the decision or takeaway the deck should support.
2. Use supplied material as the source of truth. Separate facts, estimates, and recommendations; do not invent data, quotations, citations, results, or endorsements.
3. Draft a slide-by-slide outline with one main point per slide and a logical beginning, middle, and ending. Use an executive summary or agenda only when it helps this audience.
4. Follow supplied brand rules and reference decks. Otherwise establish a restrained palette, type scale, grid, and recurring layout. Default to widescreen 16:9 unless the source deck or use case requires another ratio.
5. Choose a native/editable slide workflow when feasible. Prefer editable text, charts, and diagrams over screenshots of content.

## Build slides for live viewing

- Give each slide a takeaway title, not just a topic label. Keep body copy concise; move detail to speaker notes or an appendix when appropriate.
- Use consistent alignment, spacing, typography, and color meaning. Maintain strong contrast, legible type, and enough whitespace at the intended display size.
- Choose a chart that matches the data and label axes, units, time periods, and sources. Do not distort scales or use decorative 3D effects that obscure comparisons.
- Trace statistics to the supplied source, including population, units, time period, and any calculations or rounding. Preserve uncertainty and caveats; flag conflicting values instead of silently reconciling them.
- Use diagrams to explain relationships or processes; label arrows and unfamiliar terms. Do not add decorative imagery that competes with the message.
- Use only user-provided or properly sourced assets. Preserve attribution and licensing information when required. Never fabricate an institutional seal, logo, person, or source.
- Place concise source citations on the relevant slide and fuller references in notes or a final references slide. Ensure every factual claim can be traced to a source.
- Use speaker notes only when requested or when they materially help the presenter; keep notes distinct from slide-visible content.
- Preserve essential content and relationships when editing an existing deck. Do not silently change its approved theme, aspect ratio, or narrative intent.

## Verify before delivery

1. Render every slide and inspect a contact sheet for pacing, repetition, and visual consistency.
2. Inspect individual slides at presentation size, especially dense charts, tables, diagrams, and slides with long titles. Fix clipping, overlap, unreadable text, weak contrast, orphaned labels, and inconsistent margins; render again.
3. Check requested slide count, slide order, dimensions, text extraction, hyperlinks, notes, chart labels, and citations. Confirm key elements remain editable when editability is expected.
4. If exporting to PDF, inspect the exported PDF separately for expected page count, page size, missing fonts, links, and layout changes. Confirm both the presentation and PDF open successfully.
5. Report the deliverable, render checks performed, and any limitations. Link the final deck and any requested PDF export. Do not claim checks that were not run.

## Safety and scope

- Do not send confidential source files to an external converter or image service without authorization for that destination.
- Preserve the original deck when making substantial edits; save the result as a separate file unless the user explicitly requests replacement.
- Do not introduce unsupported claims or imply that generated visuals are real evidence.
